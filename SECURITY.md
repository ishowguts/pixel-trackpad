# Security

Pair only with an invitation shown directly on your Mac. Keep the QR private. Use **Scan QR** inside Pixel Trackpad and approve only when the phone and Mac show the same six-digit code. The scanner reads camera frames offline in memory without saving, logging, or transmitting them. It opens no external scanner or link handler and does not change system defaults. Camera access starts only after tapping Scan QR and granting permission; canceling or leaving the app closes it. Invalid QR contents remain local. Manual custom links still use the same validation and confirmation, but Android may offer other handlers for links opened outside the app.

- A direct v2 invitation contains a private LAN IP literal, listener port, SHA-256 certificate fingerprint, and random token. When no private direct endpoint exists, a hostless v1 invitation contains only the fingerprint and token and requires Bonjour discovery. Supplied hosts still require validation; malformed hosts, CLAT `192.0.0.2`, and public IPv6 addresses are rejected rather than falling back to discovery. The receiver accepts one pairing request within two minutes. The Mac must approve that request within 30 seconds and before the invitation expires. Pairing issues a distinct random credential and cannot send input.
- Android validates the invitation and checks the TLS certificate against the exact fingerprint before sending an invitation token or saved credential. Bonjour matches the instance derived from that fingerprint and still requires the exact pin. Reconnect tries a saved endpoint when available; scoped link-local endpoints require fresh discovery on every connection. Discovery records carry no credentials.
- The receiver listens on the LAN and accepts private or link-local peer addresses. Do not forward its port or expose it through a tunnel. LAN peers can attempt connections and can disrupt availability. A paired phone can control the Mac while live input is enabled.
- On an IPv6-only LAN, pairing depends on Bonjour resolving a reachable scoped IPv6 link-local endpoint. Public IPv6 addresses are not accepted as direct invitation endpoints, discovery endpoints, or inbound peers. Multicast filtering and client isolation can prevent pairing and reconnect; hostless invitations do not bypass those network limits.
- The Mac stores its identity and one phone credential in its private application support directory. Android stores its pairing and settings in app-private storage with backup disabled. **Forget Phone** revokes the credential and disconnects the active phone. If the connection drops while saving a new pairing, forget the pairing on the Mac and start again.
- Keep `.local`, private application data, QR images, signing keys, and logs containing credentials out of shared files. The receiver app stores its identity separately from the legacy command-line receiver.

The unsigned Apple Silicon preview bundles CPython and uses an ad hoc signature. Developer ID signing, notarization, Android release signing and configuration, and wider device testing remain release gates.
